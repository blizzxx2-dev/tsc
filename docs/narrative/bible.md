# Story bible — *Suture & Steel: The Malison Hours*

One page per subject (NAR-0005), Chapters III–V locations (NAR-0010), the Choir's hierarchy (NAR-0112),
the peoples (NAR-0016, NAR-0017, NAR-0018) and the saints register (NAR-0023). Every proper noun here
is logged in `names.csv`. Tone and diction: `style-guide.md`. Dates: `calendar.md`. Map: `map.md`.

---

## 1. Kessendorf, the Free City

**What it is.** A walled river-city of about thirty thousand souls on the Kessen, three days' ride from
anywhere important. It bought its freedom from a long-dead prince three hundred and twelve years ago
with a cartload of silver and a promise to keep the eastern road open; the **Kessen Charter** is still
read aloud on its anniversary, and the city still keeps the road open, mostly by hiring other people.

**Who rules.** A **Burgomaster** elected for life by the **council of guild-houses**: twelve seats,
one for each great guild (Founders, Gunsmiths, Tanners, Barber-Surgeons, Drapers, Brewers, Carters,
Chandlers, Masons, Bakers-and-Pie-men, Notaries, Vintners) plus a thirteenth seat **for the charities**,
held in year 9 of the Long Muster by the **Widow Aldegund Reiss**. The council passes writs, taxes,
quarantines and chartered offices; the Burgomaster signs things, especially on Fridays.

**What it feels like.** Money smells of tanbark and saltpetre. Every guild charges a fee for
something — including, famously, for the privilege of being injured by its own work. The **Watch**
(Captain Mauer) keeps order and dies for it; the council pays it late. Pyres outside the **east gate**
burn the autumn dead. Nobody is surprised by anything, which is what makes the Choir possible.

**Districts** (see `map.md`): the Cathedral Close; the Guildhall Square; the **Tanners' Rows** (tanneries,
lye pits, the poorest lodgings); the **gunsmiths' quarter** (proof-house, the burst-barrel yard);
the **Kilnrows** (foundries, bell-pits, powder-mills, across the river); the **Penny Stair** (pie-shops,
steps down to the water); **Wick Lane** (chandlers); the **Hospice of Saint Ildra** by the east gate.
Taverns: **the Crooked Goose** (dice, knives, Tuesdays).

**Laws that matter to a surgeon.** Practising without a guild licence is assault with a blade. The
**Inspection Decree**: any alleged mark or mutation must be certified by a licensed surgeon before the
Tribunal may act; it is two hundred years old, usually ignored, and renewed by the council in Chapter III.
Anyone accused of witchcraft is owed **a fair trial**, which in practice means one the council arranges.

## 2. The Long Muster

The **League of Free Cities** (Kessendorf and six others) called a standing levy nine years ago to hold
the eastern **Vennmark marches** against raiders, deserters and whatever comes out of the fens. The
levy has never been stood down: it has become the calendar ("the ninth year of the Long Muster"), the
tax, and the excuse. Kessendorf's share is the **Watch** (its own citizens, forty-odd men under Mauer on
campaign) and **hired companies** — pikemen, the **Ostrau crossbow company**, a few **giants** paid by the
tithe (see §7) — that the council rents by the season. The Muster is why there are always wounded,
always writs, and always somebody else's war passing through the hospice.

## 3. Saint Ildra and the Merciful Order

**Saint Ildra** was a lay-sister who kept a lamp burning in a plague-house for forty nights and carried
the keys of the dead to their families so no one else would have to open those doors. Her emblem is
**a candle and a key** (never a dove, never a bleeding heart). She is the patron of the sick poor,
night-watchers and anyone who has to tell a family.

**The Merciful Order** keeps hospices in every free city: whitewashed stone (it does not burn),
a courtyard well, boiled linen. Sisters take three vows — *refuse no one; go unarmed; keep the ledger
honest*. The Order has **no political weight**: it owns no land, sits on no council, and survives on
patrons. Its Mother Superior (at the convent in the hills) prefers her sisters out of trouble; in Chapter III
she orders Ilse home, and Ilse refuses. The Order's teaching that a sister must "never halt a soul when
it is its time to depart" is why Ilse fears the Litany — and why she ends up lying for it.

**The Hospice of Saint Ildra**, Kessendorf: a ward of twenty beds, an operating theatre that was once a
chapel, a ledger with a column for "fees, vegetable". Master Haller runs it; the Widow Reiss pays for it.

## 4. The Ash Tribunal

A **chartered office**, not a church: a court of witch-finders licensed by each free city's council
to investigate sorcery, curses and "unlicensed miracles", to hold suspects and to bring them to trial.
Its officers are **Inquisitors**; its sentence for proven witchcraft is the pyre at the east gate
(hence *Ash*). In Kessendorf the Tribunal is **Inquisitor Stroh**, two sergeants, a clerk and a seal.

**Why it frightens people.** The Tribunal does not distinguish between a prayer and a spell: anything
that works too well is evidence. It keeps paper — certificates, depositions, lists of what witnesses
saw — and paper is patient. Stroh is not cruel for pleasure; he is thorough, which is worse.

**Its weakness.** The charter must be renewed on the council roll every new year. In C. 312 the charity
seat (the Widow Reiss) simply did not sign, and the Tribunal in Kessendorf has been acting on nothing since
1 Hartung — i.e. through the whole demo (revealed in Chapter IV). The Widow renews it the morning of the trial.

## 5. The Hollow Choir (and its hierarchy, NAR-0112)

A heretic congregation that believes the canonical hours, sung perfectly and **into living flesh**,
make a curse that lives: a **Malison**. The complete **Office** — eight Hours from Matins to Compline —
sung into a whole city on Hollow Night would, they believe, "still" all suffering: a quiet night and a
perfect end. They are not devil-worshippers; there is no god behind them. **The hymns are the source.**

| Rank | Who | What they do | Tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Precentor** | Aurel Vennholt, a guild surgeon struck from the rolls by Haller twenty years ago | Composes the Hours; chooses hosts; sings the last office himself | A surgeon's apron under a grey robe; a clock-face of eight sigils burned on his chest |
| **Cantors** | a handful of trained voices | Carry an Hour to a host and "set" it (write the sigils) | Grey robes; burn-scarred throats |
| **Lay-cantors** | converts with a voice | Sing to the dead in the barrow-fields; carry messages; swallow them if caught | Silence-sigils on the chest that ignite if they confess (op2-4) |
| **Acolytes** | patrons, clerks, anyone with access | Supply money, candles, patient lists and warrants | Black gloves; a sigil under the left glove |
| — the Widow Aldegund Reiss | acolyte; the hospice's patron; the charity seat on the council | Paid for the beds and candles, knew every patient, let the Tribunal's charter lapse, signed Kreuzer's warrant | A carriage with its crest planed off (Ch1) |

**The struck eye.** Every host carries one sigil in common beside its Hour-word: **an eye with a stroke
through it** — the Choir's mark for "the watcher is closed". Emmerich (Ch1), the cantor (Ch2) and Jorg
(Ch2) all bear it; it is the continuity thread of the demo.

**The Lauds antiphon** (s2-5), the only Choir verse in the demo:
> *Who keeps the watch before the sun?* — *The one who wakes, and wakes alone.*
> *Who sings the day when day is done?* — *The one we answer, bone to bone.*

## 6. The eight Hours (the Malisons)

| Hour | Liturgical sense | As a Malison | Host |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Matins** | the night vigil | A shrouded mass with one eye that opens on a rhythm; spawns motes | Emmerich, page-boy (Ch1) |
| **Lauds** | dawn praise, sung antiphonally | Two linked bodies that answer each other; dawn flares | Jorg, standard-bearer (Ch2) |
| **Prime** | first hour; the roll of the dead read aloud | A quill that writes names into skin, stroke by stroke | Registrar Oswin Tallert (Ch3) |
| **Terce** | third hour; tongues of fire | Hexfire tongues leaping organ to organ; drinks the brand's heat | Master Haller (Ch3) |
| **Sext** | noon; the noonday demon of torpor | Fake-calm vitals, slowed hands, stone crust | Captain Mauer (Ch4) |
| **None** | the ninth hour, the hour of death | A heart-seeking burrower | Pieter, militiaman (Ch4) |
| **Vespers** | the lamp-lighting | Wick-filaments; blood turning to tallow; the field dims | Sister Ilse (Ch5) |
| **Compline** | the last office: "a quiet night and a perfect end" | Silence; steals the Litany | Inquisitor Stroh (Ch5) |

**The Litany of Stillness** is not a separate gift: it is a fragment of the old offices — the same hymn
tradition the Precentor turned into Compline. Haller learned it from Vennholt the week before he struck
him off, and taught it to Kreuzer. (Revealed s3-end; reconsidered in Ch4 and at Compline.)

## 7. Peoples and creatures (a surgeon's eye)

**The horned folk** (NAR-0016). Forest people of the Grauwald with antlered or horned skulls; not
"beast-men", not a faction of any dark power — a people with their own quarrels. They fletch arrows
with **fish-hook barbs** and keep **antler-tallies**: a notch cut in the owner's antler for every arrow
that has stuck in an enemy, so a raider's rank can be read from across a clearing. Children of
townsfolk are sometimes *born* with horn-buds ("hornfolk", natural and harmless in law); a child
whose buds come *late* is "late-turned" and belongs, in law, to the Tribunal. Never: Gor, Ungor,
herdstones, bray-shamans, "beastmen".

**The mountain-folk** (NAR-0017). Short, broad, long-lived miners of the hill country; the word
*dwarf* is used plainly in speech. Their culture is **debt**: every debt given or owed is a knot tied in
the beard, and a beard is a ledger. Nobody cuts a mountain-folk beard — a man who dies with debts
unknotted leaves them to his sons. They name mines like children, and grieve them when they fall in.
They knock "twice, then once, then twice" as a courtesy through rock. Never: grudges, grudge-books,
runes, rune-smiths, slayers, oaths of vengeance, "Karak" anything. Orsa Flintvein is the example.

**Giants (the tithe-eaters).** Three-man-high mercenaries who fight for a tithe of food rather than
coin, and swallow anything they have been told to keep safe. Thick-hided (three layers to an incision).
Gutram of the Ostrau company (Ch4).

**Gravehounds and carrion-things** (NAR-0018). *Gravehound* is a folk name for the lean corpse-eating
hounds of the barrow-fields; soldiers call any grave-robbing beast a *corpse-eater*. Their spit carries
a venom. The words are generic compounds, not unit names from any game (see `names.csv`).

**Brood-mothers.** The foresters' name for the great web-spinners of the old web-trees; they lay in
living prey. Folk say a brood-mother remembers every face that has seen her.

**Blood-thralls (the thirst).** Something in the Vennmark drinks from the living and leaves them a
bond: a week without pain after each feeding. Called *the thirst* or *the blood-debt*; its victims are
*thirsted*. Never: vampire lineage names, "Red Thirst", "Blood Kiss", "swains".

**Hexstone.** Black glass that grows in certain seams, **wet-looking, with a heartbeat** (never green,
never glowing). It sings when struck, buds flesh near it, and remembers every hand that touches it.

## 8. Chapters III–V locations (NAR-0010)

**The Kilnrows (Ch3).** The foundry quarter across the river: bell-pits, cannon-foundries, the
powder-mill on Saltpetre Lane, coal smoke that turns noon brown. Founders drink lead with their beer
(founder's colic) and bill the hospice for the lead removed. The **Founders' Guild** is the richest
seat on the council.

**The Raised Bridges (Ch3).** When the Kilnrows fever is named, the council raises the **Iron Bridge**
and the two timber bridges on their chains. The quarantine side keeps its sick; vapour-wardens in
beaked masks hang beef on poles to draw the bad air; the **Penny Stair** pie-shops become wards.

**The Guildhall of the Barber-Surgeons (Ch3).** Oak panels, portraits of dead guildmasters, the
**rolls** (every licensed hand in the city, and one name struck through twenty years ago). The licence
vote on Kreuzer is held here; Terce burns it to the stone.

**The Vennmark marches (Ch4).** Fen and heath four days east: peat, reeds, rain that lasts three days,
a field hospital of wet canvas, camp-followers' lines, a quartermaster's wagon that holds the council's
pay-chest. The marches are where the League sends its hired wars and its awkward people.

**Hollow Night processions (Ch5).** At dusk the streets empty; doors are hung with iron nails and dried
rue. The Choir walks openly for the first time — grey-robed lines with lanterns high, singing the
Vespers verse, converging on the hospice and then on the Tribunal court.

**The Tribunal court (Ch5).** An old hall by the Cathedral Close, rebuilt on the cellars of an older
chapel; the cells are cut into rock, thin enough that a mountain-folk courtesy can be heard through the
wall. Beneath the cellars, the crypt where the Precentor sings Compline.
