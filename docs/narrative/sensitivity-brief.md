# Sensitivity brief (NAR-0027)

Rules for depicting the hard things this game is about. Applies to scenes, operations, barks, codex,
case notes, art direction notes and store copy. External readers use this brief as their checklist
(Ch1–2: NAR-0028; Ch3–5: NAR-0031…0033 — `docs/handoff/NAR/`).

## 1. Plague and disease
- The sick are **people with names and trades** (Matthis Kolb, tanner's man — never "the vagrant").
- Poverty is a condition, never a character flaw: no line blames the poor for their disease.
- Folk cures and council quarantines may be mocked; **symptoms, suffering and death may not**.
- Plague imagery (buboes, rot, grubs) is shown on the operating field only — clinical, not lingering
  for disgust. No corpse piles as set-dressing in close-up; carts of dead are background at most.
- Avoid real-world pandemic slogans or modern public-health language.

## 2. Children in peril (Emmerich, Liesl, Jakob; the newborn)
- A child is never shown **in pain outside the operating table**, never tortured, never killed.
  Every child patient in the campaign survives a won operation; a lost operation is framed as the
  surgeon's failure to retry, not a child's death scene (failure scenes stay generic and short).
- The Inspection Decree's menace comes from **institutions** (the council, the Tribunal), never from
  the child. Liesl's examination is described through Kreuzer's eye and her mother's fear, not her body.
- No sexualised or romantic framing of anyone under age, ever.

## 3. Religious persecution
- The Ash Tribunal is a **legal institution** doing harm by procedure; its victims are not guilty
  "really". Accusation is never proof, and the game never rewards accusing someone.
- The Merciful Order and ordinary faith are portrayed with respect; hypocrisy is shown in institutions,
  not in believers. The Hollow Choir is a sect with a mortal leader and a human motive — no real-world
  religion, liturgy text or minority is mapped onto it. Hymn verses are original.
- Flagellants and penitents (Ch3) are treated with dignity: their pain is theirs, not a joke.

## 4. Torture and execution
- **Never shown on screen; only implied.** No witch-pricking, racking or burning is depicted; the pyre
  is smoke on the horizon and a sentence read aloud. The trial (Ch5) is argued with evidence.
- Prisoners (the cantor) are patients first; the camera never lingers on restraints.

## 5. Disability and amputation
- An amputation is a **life saved**, not a tragedy ending: Berthold gets "a peg and a cushion" and
  drives a cart again. No "better dead than maimed" lines from sympathetic characters.
- Scars, burns and missing limbs are never used as signs of villainy. The Precentor is not disfigured
  to mark him evil; his sigil-scars are the curse's, not his character's.
- Haller's burned hands (Ch3–5) change his work, not his worth.

## 6. Other care areas
- **Addiction and the thirst (Margit, Ch4):** her choice is hers; the script does not moralise.
- **Obstetrics (Rosina, Ch5):** hopeful, respectful, medically plausible for the period (consultant sign-off).
- **Assisted death (Compline):** argued on both sides; never endorsed as the "right" ending, never caricatured.
- **Self-harm (Brother Ansgar):** no instructional detail; framed with compassion.

## 7. Content notes for players
Players can turn on **"Show content notes"** (options) to see a card before Chapters IV and V (NAR-0034).
Store descriptors: `content-descriptors.md` (NAR-0029).

## Reviewer checklist
For each scene/op: (1) Who is the joke on? (2) Is any child in pain outside the table? (3) Is torture
shown rather than implied? (4) Is disability used as a sign of evil or a fate worse than death?
(5) Does any patient lack a name where one is possible? Log every note: id · line · note · accept/decline · reason.
