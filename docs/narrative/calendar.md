# Calendar and dating convention (NAR-0006)

Used by scenes, codex, case notes, letters and store copy. Timeline of the campaign: `timeline.md`.

## Eras
- **Vernacular (what people say):** *“the ninth year of the Long Muster”*. The Long Muster is the
  standing levy the League of Free Cities called against the eastern Vennmark marches; it has never
  been stood down, so everyone counts from it. The Prologue opens in **year 9 of the Long Muster**.
- **Formal (what documents say):** years of the **Kessen Charter** (the city's charter of freedom),
  written *“in the 312th year of the Charter”* or *“C. 312”*. Year 9 of the Muster = **C. 312**.
  Writs, certificates, guild rolls, the council roll and case notes use the Charter year; people
  speaking use the Muster.
- Never: “AD”, “IC”, real saints' feast dates, or weekday names beyond the ordinary seven.

## Months
Twelve months of thirty days, plus **the Five Nameless Days** at year's end, when no contract may be
signed and the courts do not sit. The new year falls on **1 Hartung**; chartered offices (the Tribunal's
included) must be renewed on the council roll by then.

| # | Month | Meaning | Season / what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hartung** | the hard (frost) month | New year; charters renewed; river freezes. **Chapters I–II** |
| 2 | **Hornung** | the horn month (antlers shed) | Horned folk move down from the Grauwald |
| 3 | **Lenzing** | the lengthening month | Thaw; the Kilnrows fire the spring pour. **Chapter III** |
| 4 | **Saatmond** | the sowing moon | Companies march; rain. **Chapter IV**; ends on **Hollow Night** (**Chapter V**) |
| 5 | **Wonnemond** | the joy moon | Spring caravans; the morning after Hollow Night (s5-end) |
| 6 | **Brachet** | the fallow-ploughing month | |
| 7 | **Heuet** | the hay month | |
| 8 | **Ernting** | the harvest month | |
| 9 | **Scheiding** | the parting month | Guild licence votes are held (normally) |
| 10 | **Gilbhart** | the yellowing month | |

The names are historical German month names (public domain); none is used by another fantasy
setting's calendar that we know of (see `names.csv`).

## Hours
Time of day is told by bells and the **canonical hours** sung at the cathedral — the same names as the
Malisons, which is why the Choir's Office is so frightening to Kessendorf:
Matins (small hours) · Lauds (dawn) · Prime (first hour of day) · Terce (mid-morning) · Sext (noon) ·
None (mid-afternoon, “the ninth hour”) · Vespers (lamp-lighting) · Compline (the last office, before sleep).
Scene captions use plain words (“dawn”, “the small hours”) except where the Hour itself is the point.

## Hollow Night
**The last night of Saatmond** (30 Saatmond, the eve of 1 Wonnemond). One night in the year when, folk
say, the moon is hollow and anything born, sworn or buried belongs to whoever is singing. Doors are
hung with iron nails and dried rue; midwives will not deliver; the streets are empty after dusk.
In year 9 of the Long Muster, the Hollow Choir chose it to finish the Office (Chapter V).
Never: Hexensnacht, Geheimnisnacht, Walpurgis (real, but too loaded), or two moons.

## Examples
- Prologue caption: *“The Free City of Kessendorf. Winter, in the ninth year of the Long Muster.”*
- Hornchild certificate (s3-2): *“Examined this third day of Lenzing, in the 312th year of the Charter…”*
- Case note header: *“14 Hartung, C. 312. Jost, drover. Knife wounds.”*
- Charter lapse (s4-8): *“It lapsed at the new year”* = 1 Hartung, C. 312 — i.e. *before* the Prologue.
