# Warhammer Fantasy research for *Suture & Steel: The Malison Hours*: tone, medicine, corruption, and IP to avoid

**Purpose.** This document collects what makes Warhammer Fantasy (the Old World tabletop setting, Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay [WFRP] 1e–4e, Total War: Warhammer) *feel* the way it does, so we can build an **original** grimdark surgery-action game with the same tone. Nothing here should ship as-is. Every GW-specific name, place, god, faction, disease, and mechanic is listed in §7 with a direction for an original replacement.

**Sources.** Most lore comes from ~100 pages of the Warhammer Fantasy wiki (`warhammerfantasy.fandom.com`, which mirrors the Lexicanum-style lore pages), the WFRP 1e wiki (`wfrp1e.fandom.com`), a WFRP 4e critical-wounds reference sheet, Total War coverage, and Wikipedia for the real history behind it. Links are inline.

## 1. Tone and aesthetic pillars

**The core promise.** WFRP's 1986 subtitle, *"A Grim World of Perilous Adventure"*, set it up as the grimy cousin of heroic D&D. Reviewers describe the mix as "European history—the Holy Roman Empire in particular, Moorcockian cosmology, humour, grim and perilous feel, disease and damnation, and mud and shit underfoot" ([Reviews from R'lyeh](http://rlyehreviews.blogspot.com/2022/09/warhammer-fantasy-roleplay-iv.html?m=1); [Fandomentals primer](https://www.thefandomentals.com/warhammer-fantasy-roleplay-primer/)).

### Pillar A: A Holy Roman Empire pastiche that is fragmented, legalistic, and faithful
- **Elected emperor, squabbling electors.** An emperor rules through provincial electors, and imperial law survives only if the electors choose to enforce it. Big cities are semi-autonomous city-states whose government "varies from city to city", some run by elected mercantile families ([The Empire](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/The_Empire)). Imperial law "concerns itself mostly with revenues, security..., the regulation of sorcery, and the rooting out of Chaos cults". That is exactly the pressure a charity surgeon lives under.
- **Religious pluralism with teeth.** Northern "country" gods sit beside southern "town" gods. There has been sectarian civil war (an "Age of Three Emperors"), and some cults are banned outright (ibid.).
- **Free merchant cities.** Marienburg bought its independence and is run by a *Directorate* of merchant houses above a debating-club parliament. It has riots against anti-labour-guild laws and a huge smuggling economy ([Marienburg](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Marienburg)). This is the closest model for our free city, Kessendorf.

### Pillar B: Squalor as the default
- The 1e rules say it plainly: "rats are more numerous than people in most towns"; disease comes from "polluted water supplies, open sewers, and badly-preserved food", and "the main risk of infection for adventurers comes from bites and other wounds" ([WFRP1e Disease](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Disease)).
- Nuln, the "city of high culture", still chokes on foundry smog and ash. The Gunnery School fires four to six times a day and rings a warning bell before each volley. Behind every clean façade the servants' entrances are "just as foul as anywhere else" ([Nuln](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nuln)).

### Pillar C: Black humour that sits beside real horror
The setting keeps undercutting its own misery with jokes:
- The Galloping Trots is called "Rumster's Revenge" after penny meat pies, so visitors end up "spending a penny twice" ([Galloping Trots](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Galloping_Trots)). Ratte Fever is nicknamed "Pie Fever" because so many pasties hold rat meat, and the Altdorf folk cure is self-flagellation ([Ratte Fever](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Ratte_Fever)).
- Official medicine for the Black Plague: hoist a side of beef up a flagpole for two days, then bury it "together with the 'unwholesome vapours'" ([WFRP1e Disease](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Disease)).
- Mothers warn that nose-picking or pulling faces will "make a mutant of you!" ([Mutant](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutant)).
- Some nurses of the mercy goddess slap each other in greeting ("slappers"). Patients who fall for their nurses are "dove fancying", and those men get assigned the oldest sister on staff ([Cult of Shallya](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Cult_of_Shallya)).
- A death-cult priest famously performs a full funeral in under a minute while fleeing orcs ([Cult of Morr](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Cult_of_Morr)). A death spell curses a warrior who never smiled to "laugh until his sides ruptured", and the tellers argue over whose fate was worse, his or "the servants who had to clean up the mess" ([Lore of Death](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Lore_of_Death)).
- **Takeaway for us:** keep the jokes coming from institutions and folk belief (guild fees, quack cures, pie vendors, bureaucratic witch-law). Never joke about the patient's suffering itself.

### Pillar D: Art direction drawn from the Old Masters
- John Blanche defined the look. His references were Dürer, Bosch, Rembrandt and da Vinci; he took studio artists to galleries so they would see "it was all there" in a Dürer or a Bosch ([Wikipedia: John Blanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Blanche); [Sprites & Dice](https://spritesanddice.com/features/ode-john-blanche-and-power-art/)).
- For us, these are public-domain references with no IP risk:
  - **Dürer:** engraving crosshatch, the *Knight, Death and the Devil* mood.
  - **Bruegel:** crowded peasant panoramas and *The Triumph of Death*.
  - **Bosch:** hybrid grotesques that suit curse-growths.
  - **Holbein:** the *Dance of Death* woodcuts, for results and death screens.
  - **Surgical woodcuts:** the *Wound Man* of Gersdorff's *Feldtbuch der Wundartzney* (1517/1519), a figure covered in weapon cuts, pustules, and venomous bites who stands "defiantly alive". That makes it a perfect title or briefing motif ([Wikipedia: Wound Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wound_Man)).
- **Costume:** Landsknecht slashed-and-puffed doublets, halberds and early handguns. Imperial state troops fight as halberd blocks with handgunner and swordsman "detachments", and Tilean crossbow mercenaries are hired in ([Imperial Army](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_Army)).

### Pillar E: Faith as double-edged
Flagellants read every plague as divine judgement and wander between villages whipping themselves ([Flagellant](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Flagellant); [Black Plague](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Plague)). When a comet destroyed a decadent city, preachers said the god "cut them away as a surgeon might slice open a boil to drain the pus of corruption" ([Mordheim](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mordheim)). The canon already uses surgery as a metaphor for purgation. **Our twist:** Kreuzer's surgery is mercy, and the pyre is the false cure.

**Pillars we propose for *Suture & Steel*:**
1. **Mercy in a merciless world.** Saving a life is the only victory, and it is always provisional.
2. **The body as a battlefield.** Wounds are sorted by what caused them: blade, blunt, bite, arrow, shot, fire.
3. **Every miracle is evidence.** Skill that looks supernatural draws suspicion.
4. **Gallows humour aimed at institutions.**
5. **Material honesty.** Tallow, brass, gut thread, leeches, woodcut labels, candle smoke.
6. **Institutions as antagonists.** The guild, the inquisition and the council are as dangerous as any monster.

## 2. Medicine in the setting

### 2.1 Who heals whom
| Healer | What the canon says | Use for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Barber-surgeon** | "Painful but effective healing to the common folk." Carries scalpels up to "enormous bone saws"; dentistry is "the most hated of their avocations". Common as ship's surgeons and battlefield "field surgeons". Guilds are regulated because barbers "hold razors to the throats of honest citizens". **Practising without a guild licence counts as assault with a weapon, a capital crime.** Guilds are run by a few families; some force members to pass on what they hear while shaving to the authorities, organised crime, or cults. Unlicensed "blacklegs" are hunted down. Most refuse to operate at full witch-moon because "the razor slips" ([Barber-Surgeon](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Barber-Surgeon)). | Kreuzer's licence is permanent leverage. Master Haller's guild politics and a blackleg rival are ready-made plots. |
| **Physician** | University- or guild-trained; medicine is "relatively new and still distrusted". **Cadaver study is forbidden** because of necromancy fears and the death-cult's safeguards, so human anatomy is hard to learn. Quacks sell "miracle cure-alls". Some physicians bribe jailers for test subjects. Guild fees are ruinous. Physicians swear an oath to the mercy goddess to "preserve life, never take it", and they dismiss wise women's cures as "coincidence" ([Physician](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Physician)). | Kreuzer's anatomical knowledge is itself suspect, because where did he learn it? That is a secret-dissection backstory. |
| **Mercy-goddess temples** | Every city and major town has a whitewashed temple that "double[s] as hospitals for those too poor to afford a physician" ([Shallya](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Shallya)). Plan: a courtyard with a fountain of the goddess's tears, the infirmary at one end, stone rather than wood so it survives fires, a separate birthing wing, sometimes a madhouse, and an orphanage raising future nurses. White robes are made to be "washed by boiling". Rules: never refuse healing, "never halt a soul when it is time for it to depart", go unarmed. A radical sect teaches that **mutants can be healed**, and remote hospices are rumoured to shelter them; "the Witch Hunters would dearly love to discover the truth" ([Cult of Shallya](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Cult_of_Shallya)). | The Hospice of Saint Ildra and Sister Ilse. The rule "never halt a soul when it is time" gives Ilse a doctrinal reason to fear the Litany. |
| **Hedge wizards and wise women** | "Most... dwell in villages, making their livings as healers. In fact, most claim not to be magic-users at all, instead professing to be **apothecaries or herbalists**." They flee when a witch hunter comes to town ([Hedge Wizard](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Hedge_Wizard)). | **Exactly the accusation against an "apothecary-surgeon" with a time-slowing gift.** Stroh's suspicion is canonically reasonable. |
| **Rat-clan flesh-shapers** | A monster faction of "Master Moulders" builds war-beasts "through breeding, mutation, and surgical alteration", and uses mutagenic salves to heal ([Clan Moulder](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Clan_Moulder)). | Evil surgeon as the protagonist's mirror, but make ours original (see §7). |

**Two canon laws worth adapting:**
- **The mutation-inspection decree.** An old imperial decree requires any alleged mutation to be examined by an accredited barber-surgeon or physician before the mutant is executed. It has never been repealed, and it is "almost always ignored" ([Barber-Surgeon](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Barber-Surgeon)). This hands us a whole forensic discipline: Kreuzer's signature can save or condemn someone.
- **The fair-trial rule.** People accused of witchcraft must get "a fair trial". Watchmen avoid arresting them because "who wants to risk being cursed?" ([Colleges of Magic](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Colleges_of_Magic)). That explains Captain Mauer's reluctance.

### 2.2 How WFRP turns healing into rules (mechanics to translate)
- **1e Heal Wounds.** First aid can only *stop* bleeding in the critically wounded. The patient still "will die unless successfully treated by a character with Surgery skill within 24 hours" ([Heal Wounds](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Heal_Wounds)). *Game use:* field triage stabilises, and the real operation happens later at the hospice. That is the Trauma Team triage-then-operate loop.
- **1e Surgery.** Six procedures: treat heavy wounds, stop wound loss, heal severe wounds (a two-hour operation that the patient dies from if it fails), set limbs (within one week or the limb is crippled for good), treat amputation ("under no circumstances can a surgeon replace a severed limb"; cauterise instead), and speed recovery. Operating "in a damp, dark, underground cave while hand-to-hand combat rages around him" drops success to 5–10% ([Surgery](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Surgery)). *Game use:* the operating environment modifies difficulty (Chapter 4 field surgery).
- **1e Cure Disease.** An Int test modified by the patient's Toughness; "critical failures should have very drastic outcomes – possibly even the death of the patient" ([Cure Disease](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Cure_Disease)).
- **1e critical hits are split by what caused them:** sharp weapons, blunt weapons, teeth and claws, arrows and bolts, firearms, falling and crushing, fire and energy ([Critical Hits](https://wfrp1e.fandom.com/wiki/Critical_Hits)). Examples: "severs your opponent's carotid artery... bleed to death in D4 rounds"; bone splinters that "sever arteries and poke through the skin"; "horribly burned... loses 1 W per round... as shock begins to set in". *This matches our wound taxonomy and can drive procedurally generated patients.*
- **4e critical wounds are split by hit location** (head, arm, body, leg; d100 tables). Several are marked **"Surgery"** and keep reopening until they are surgically closed:
  - *Gaping Wound* and *Major Chest Wound:* every new hit to the area adds more bleeding "as the tears reopen".
  - *Arterial Damage:* +2 bleeding per hit until surgery.
  - *Gut Wound:* causes a **Festering Wound**.
  - *Internal Bleeding:* bleeding "that can only be removed through Surgery" plus **Blood Rot**.
  - Also: broken bones (minor/major), torn muscles, and amputations graded by difficulty. Tooth counts even differ by race: losing half your teeth means 16 for humans, 18 for elves, 20 for dwarfs and halflings ([WFRP4 Critical Hits Reference Sheet](https://bindslet.dk/rpg/4e_Critical_Reference_Sheet_1.04.pdf)).
  - *Game use:* complications that re-bleed until you suture.
- **4e conditions:** *Bleeding* (lose 1 Wound per round, with a cumulative chance of death), *Poisoned*, *Ablaze*, *Stunned*, *Unconscious*, and so on ([WFRP4e wikidot: Conditions](http://wfrp4e.wikidot.com/rules:conditions)). Bleeding also penalises resistance to Festering Wound, Minor Infection and Blood Rot (ibid.). *Game use:* stack status effects on the patient that feed the vitals drain.
- **4e diseases are built from modular symptoms.** Each disease lists contraction, incubation, duration and symptoms drawn from a shared pool: **Blight, Buboes, Convulsions, Coughs & Sneezes, Fever, Flux, Gangrene, Lingering, Malaise, Nausea, Pox, Wounded** ([WFRP4e Random Disease generator, citing core pp. 188–189](https://pacomiscelaneousstuff.blogspot.com/2020/03/wfrp-4th-edition-random-disease.html)).
  - *Buboes* are lanced with a Heal (Surgery) test; a failed lance causes a Festering Wound.
  - *Gangrene* worsens into a Festering Wound, and a very bad failure into Blood Rot.
  - *Coughs & Sneezes* force everyone nearby to test hourly (per a 4e player aid, [pdfcoffee](https://pdfcoffee.com/wfpa322-wfrp-4e-player-aid-pdf-free.html)).
  - **Design lesson:** build our ailments out of symptom modules (see §8, "Symptom Loom").

### 2.4 Mutation, corruption, and "treating" mutants
- **How people see it.** Mutation is "the most personal of the threats": "one can never tell who hides some strange corruption beneath a baggy shirt" ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)). Mutants are burned at the stake as popular spectacle, and mass burnings draw crowds "from miles around" ([Mutant](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutant)).
- **Treatment.** "Some afflictions are treatable, requiring a slash of a knife or a firm twist of the flesh with pliers. Though the physical presence of the mutation is gone, the corruption isn't... some new affliction will appear" ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)). A physician-turned-torturer, "Bloody Maria", sets up in a slum night-market "hacking off tendrils, eyestalks, bird claws, beaks" for coin ([Physician](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Physician)). A few physicians are "open-minded enough (or venal enough)" to remove mutated parts ([Mutant](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutant)).
- **Folk prevention.** Pregnant women rub animal fat on the belly weekly, or make up a missed week with "a quart of ox blood mixed with goat milk". Midwives try to **slow births during the witch-moon night** until the sun rises. Children who seem normal but "turn" later ("turnskins") are abandoned in the woods ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)).
- **Mutations that make good operations:**
  - *Chaos Organ:* a cancerous cyst that preserves its host; if the host dies, it "tears free... sprouts limbs and waddles around".
  - *Intelligent Cyst:* has its own goals and sometimes takes control.
  - *Unbelievable Tumour:* 10–100 lb, grown in days.
  - *Vestigial/Parasitic Twin*.
  - *Blood Substitution:* blood replaced by acid, centipedes, leeches, mice, molten metal, tar, wax, vines...
  - *Crystalline Body:* cut crystal "rots away into a puddle of dung after one to ten minutes".
  - *Inside Out* ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)).
- **Staged exposure model.** A dwarf physician's three-stage "warpstone sickness":
  1. Under an hour of exposure: nausea, nosebleeds, coughing, temporary blindness.
  2. An hour to a day: **false euphoria and "recovery"**, then minor mutations that are reversible if nothing worsens within a week.
  3. Over a day: major mutation, no cure ([Warpstone](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Warpstone)).
  - The false-recovery stage is dramatic gold: the patient seems fine, then relapses.
- **Who resists.** Halflings are immune to mutation, dwarfs are highly resistant, and elves are resistant except to plague-god diseases ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation); [Dwarf](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Dwarf); [Elves](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Elves)).
- **Tainted books.** A cult printed a murder-catalogue chapbook in warpstone ink, and its readers went berserk ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)). **Corruption can spread through printed or sung text.** That fits a coven called a *Choir*.

### 2.5 Plague doctors
Beaked plague doctors are **not** a signature Warhammer element; the setting prefers flagellants, mercy sisters and rat-catchers. Historically, the costume is credited to Charles de Lorme (1619): waxed robe, glass eyepieces, a beak stuffed with aromatics (roses, camphor, vinegar sponges) against "miasma", and a cane for examining at arm's length. It was popularised by Paul Fürst's 1656 engraving, and only two genuine masks survive ([Wikipedia: Plague doctor costume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_doctor_costume)).
- **Recommendation:** use the beak for a *minor* faction, such as council-hired "vapour-wardens" who follow miasma theory and prescribe beef-on-a-pole-style rituals. Kreuzer then wins by doing better medicine than they do.
- **Real surgical history to borrow (public domain):**
  - Ambroise Paré, a barber-surgeon, dropped boiling-oil cautery for gunshot wounds in favour of an egg-yolk, rose-oil and turpentine dressing.
  - Paré revived ligature of arteries during amputation, using a "crow's-beak" clamp.
  - He described phantom limb pain.
  - His motto: "I dressed him, and God healed him" ([Wikipedia: Ambroise Paré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambroise_Par%C3%A9)).
  - These map directly onto Cautery Brand vs Gut Thread choices.

## 3. Chaos and corruption as raw material for the Malison (adapt, don't copy)

**The canon premise.** Chaos is magic fed by emotion. Its gods "derive from... the darker emotions and deepest psychological drives" of mortals ([Chaos](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Chaos)).
- **The plague god** embodies despair and decay. He is a "loving grandfather" who takes away the fear of death in exchange for the soul. His sacred number is 7 ([Nurgle](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nurgle)).
- **The change god** embodies hope, ambition, scheming and sorcery. His number is 9 ([Chaos Gods](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Chaos_Gods); [Tzeentch](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Tzeentch)).
- The two are sworn rivals: stasis-in-rot against endless change.

**What to keep abstractly:**
1. **A curse that offers relief.** Plague cults recruit "among the sick and desperate" in **"hospices, leper colonies, and filthy urban slums"**, and whisper "promises of deliverance" at sickbeds "behind the backs of his physicians". "The greatest coup a cult... can achieve is to corrupt... a doctor of medicine, folk-healer, or priest" ([Nurgle](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nurgle)).
   - **Hollow Choir hook:** the Choir sings to the dying in our own hospice and offers them *Stillness without pain*. That is a dark mirror of Kreuzer's Litany.
2. **A cult with a charitable front.** Cults hide in "orphanages, or even established temples", with an inner coven of acolytes around a hidden magus who is too marked to show his face ([Chaos Cult](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Chaos_Cult)). The fallen-physician trope, a society of healers used as a cult front, exists in canon ([Nurgle: Festus](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nurgle)). **Avoid copying it outright.** If we use a "fallen healer", make the motive and structure ours: for example, a choirmaster who lost a child on the operating table.
3. **Staged progression.** Seven or eight monthly stages of the rot-plague map naturally onto eight canonical hours. A Malison could advance "one hour" in a patient between chapters.
4. **Splitting when cut wrongly.** The change god's daemons, when wounded, collapse and divide into two smaller copies ([Pink Horror](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Pink_Horror)). This is like Trauma Center's *Triti* strain multiplying under bad technique (Trauma Center wiki "GUILT" article; local copy at `scratchpad/wiki/tc/GUILT.txt`).
5. **Parasites that grow inside a host and eat their way out.** Foot-high daemons grow inside a greater daemon's rotting flesh ([Nurgling](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nurgling)). Our existing *Grub* entity fits here.
6. **Death is not the end of the curse.** The *Chaos Organ* walks away from a dead host. A patient who dies in surgery could release a "Malison remnant" for the next operation ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)).
7. **Corruption as a threshold.** In WFRP 4e, corruption builds up from exposure (Endurance or Cool tests; some sources corrupt you even if you pass). **When it exceeds Toughness Bonus + Willpower Bonus, you test for mutation.** "Dark Deals" let a player take corruption in exchange for a reroll ([Ill Met by Morrslieb review](https://illmetbymorrslieb.wordpress.com/2023/11/07/review-fourth-edition-rulebook-part-4-additional-rules/); [PhilGamer](https://philgamer.wordpress.com/2019/05/03/lets-study-warhammer-fantasy-roleplay-4e-part-3c-corruption-disease-and-infection/)).
   - **Adaptation:** a hidden "Taint" value on the patient that rises during Malison phases. A second Litany use per operation could be a Dark-Deal analogue: allowed, but it raises Kreuzer's personal *Whisper*, a suspicion and corruption meter.
8. **Corruption as territory.** In Total War, corruption types compete zero-sum across provinces, with thresholds up to 100 that damage public order and cause attrition ([TW Wiki: Corruption](https://totalwarwarhammer.fandom.com/wiki/Corruption); [How Corruption Works](https://wiki.totalwar.com/w/How_Corruption_Works_in_Total_War:_WARHAMMER)).
   - **Adaptation:** a Kessendorf district map between chapters. Each successful operation pushes back "Choir hymnal" influence in a district, and districts over a threshold send harder patients.
9. **Plagues built from symptoms.** Total War: Warhammer III's plague faction brews plagues from **three adjacent symptoms** on a web (Pox, Buboes, Ague, Rot, Palsy...). A used symptom goes on cooldown, and randomisation makes one symptom "blessed" (stronger) ([Bell of Lost Souls](https://www.belloflostsouls.net/2021/11/total-war-warhammer-3-nurgle-campaign-mechanics-unveiled.html); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/total-war-warhammer-3/unique-mechanics/z8f8df)).
   - **Adaptation:** in canon the Choir writes each Malison as a three-*verse* hymn. In challenge mode, curses are generated from our own symptom-verses.

**The eight canonical hours as a design skeleton (real liturgy, public domain):** Matins (night vigil), Lauds (dawn praise), Prime (first hour, start of work), Terce (third hour, mid-morning), Sext (noon), None (ninth hour, mid-afternoon, traditionally the hour of Christ's death), Vespers (evening lamp-lighting), Compline (night prayer "for a quiet night and a perfect end", followed by the Great Silence) ([Wikipedia: Canonical hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_hours)). Each hour gets a theme, a lighting grade, and a signature mechanic. The full table is in §8.

## 5. Magic: the eight winds, hedge magic, and mapping to curses and the Litany

**Canon structure.** Magic is one Chaos-energy that splits into eight coloured "winds" when it enters the world. Licensed wizards may use only one wind; mixing them makes corrupting "dark magic" ([Winds of Magic](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Winds_of_Magic)). Unlicensed spellcasting is witchcraft, punished by burning ([Colleges of Magic](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Colleges_of_Magic)). Apprentices who show "the first sign of instability" quietly disappear ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)). Hedge wizards draw "low-power... ambient magic" from ordinary objects without any theory of winds, and drift toward dark magic without noticing ([Hedge Wizard](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Hedge_Wizard)).

**Mapping ideas.** Do not use a coloured eight-wind system as-is. The *concept* of eight emotional or elemental currents is common, but GW's colour-college structure is distinctive. We already have an eight-part structure in the canonical hours. **Let the hours replace the winds**: each Malison is a hymn sung *at* an hour, not a wind.

| Canon wind (theme) | Canon spell flavour | Our curse or ailment idea |
|---|---|---|
| Fire | fireballs, burning heads | **Hexfire burns** (existing): burns that re-ignite unless salved. The Terce "tongues of flame". |
| Heavens / stars | fate, ill-omen, lightning | **Omen-sigils** that predict where the next laceration will open (telegraphed attacks). |
| Metal / alchemy | transmutation; the Gold college seeks a "spiritual alkahest" to burn out taint ([Mutation](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mutation)) | Embedded shot that **transmutes** (lead to quicksilver to gold-rot). A curative "alkahest" Tincture as an unlockable. |
| Beasts | wildform, bestial rage | **Turning curse:** fur, horn-buds, teeth erupting in a wound. |
| Life | healing and growth; "Flesh to Stone"; runaway vitality ([Lore of Life](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Lore_of_Life)) | **Overgrowth** (tumours, vines in the veins, wounds healing *wrong*) and **petrification** crusts. |
| Light | purification, banishing | The Choir's enemy. Maybe the source of Saint's Salve's blessed glow. |
| Death | **time, transition, memory; the hourglass is its emblem** ([Lore of Death](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Lore_of_Death)). An undetectable "heart attack" squeeze; a limb-deadening tomb-robber's curse; a spell that lets the dying finish one last act ([Lore of Death](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Lore_of_Death)) | **Heart-grip curse** (None). Limb-deadening curse on a grave-robber. The Compline "perfect end" euthanasia curse. |
| Shadow / illusion | concealment, deceiving minds | Curses that **hide from the Scrying Lens**, decoy "phantom wounds", fog on the viewport. |
| Dark magic (mixed) | corrupting and unstable | The Choir's craft: "polyphony" that mixes hours, as in the final boss. |

**The Litany of Stillness (time-slow) in-world:**
- **Canon parallels:** the death wind already owns time and the hourglass, and hedge healers pass off magic as apothecary skill. So Stroh's suspicion is fair *within the genre*.
- **Suggested original framing:** the Litany is a *prayer* on the **Five Wounds**. The five-pointed star has been both a Christian emblem of Christ's five wounds (Gawain's pentangle) and an occult sign ([Wikipedia: Pentagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagram)). That ambiguity is the drama: the same gesture reads as devotion to Sister Ilse and as a witch's sign to Stroh.
- **Cost:** after each use, a patch of Kreuzer's palm goes numb and insensible. That ties directly to historical **witch-pricking**. Prickers stabbed suspects with bodkins looking for a "witch's mark" that "would not feel pain or bleed", and fraudulent prickers used retractable or blunt tools ([Wikipedia: Pricking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pricking); [Witch's mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch%27s_mark)). This gives us a tense inquisition minigame in which Kreuzer must hide or explain the mark.

## 6. Society and campaign hooks

**Useful institutions (analogues to build):**
- **Witch hunters come in four kinds** ([Witch Hunter](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Witch_Hunter)):
  1. *Chartered mercenaries* carry a noble's written mandate that ends at the noble's borders ("limited jurisdiction"). Some are bribed and some help with land-grabs.
  2. *Devout templars* are usually attached to the state church.
  3. *Renegades* hunt alone.
  4. *Impostors:* **impersonating a witch hunter is a capital offence.**
  - Tactics: hunters "befriend" suspects to watch them, recruit locals as informants, and interrogate under torture. They wear lead chains as a charm "against witchcraft" and carry silvered blades and pistols. Their followers are "crazed and self-mutilated" rabble.
  - **Stroh design:** is he a *chartered* agent of Kessendorf's council (limited jurisdiction, open to legal challenge) or a *devout* zealot? A mid-game reveal that his charter has lapsed makes a good twist.
- **The mercy temple is politically weak on purpose.** Its high temple "wields almost no political influence, which is welcomed" ([Cult of Shallya](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Cult_of_Shallya)). So Ilse's order cannot protect Kreuzer from the inquisition.
- **Guild politics:** family-controlled surgeons' guilds, blackleg bounties, information-brokering, and ruinous physicians' guild fees (§2.1).
- **Mercenaries and war fronts:** professional mercenary bands for hire, crossbow specialists, deserters setting up quietly ([Imperial Army](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Imperial_Army)). Borderlands include forests full of beast-folk, haunted eastern marches with ghoul famines and undead ([Stirland](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Stirland); [Sylvania](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Sylvania); [Ghoul](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Ghoul)), a cursed ruin that draws shard-hunters ([Mordheim](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Mordheim)), and a foundry city making guns ([Nuln](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Nuln)).
- **The witch-moon:** on two nights a year both moons are full, the streets empty, and people hang charms. Its light "can corrupt flesh, mutate the unborn, and inspire madness" ([Morrslieb](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Morrslieb)). **Our version:** one annual "Hollow Night" when the Choir sings openly. This could be the Chapter 5 set piece.
- **Quarantine politics:** one city survived the great plague by raising its bridges ([Black Plague](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Plague)). Another city burned its poor quarter to stop a pox ([Red Pox](https://warhammerfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Pox)). **The council may order the hospice burned.**

**Five-chapter arc (suggested):**
1. **Ch1 "The Hour of Matins" (built).** The hospice at night: a tavern knife, a barbed shaft, powder burns, pestilent humours, then Matins. Hooks: guild licence pressure from Haller; Stroh arrives "to observe".
2. **Ch2 "The Hour of Lauds" (demo finale): monsters and magic reach the walls.** A convoy ambushed in the forest brings beast-folk gorings, a ghoul-scratched gravedigger, a troll-blooded sellsword, and a hedge-witch's curse-knot. Stroh invokes the **inspection decree** and makes Kreuzer examine an alleged "turning" child. The first pricking scene. The Lauds boss (an antiphonal pair). Cliffhanger for the demo: the Choir's hymn is heard in the ward at dawn, and the child's horn-buds match a sigil in a patient from Ch1.
3. **Ch3 "Prime and Terce": the foundry quarter and quarantine.** A powder-mill explosion, lead poisoning, hexfire. A plague breaks out and the council raises the bridges. Flagellants march; vapour-wardens hang beef from poles. Guild politics come to a head (Haller's licence vote).
4. **Ch4 "Sext and None": the field.** The city's mercenaries march east. Field surgery in bad conditions (1e's cave penalty made into gameplay) with tomb-rot, a vampire's swain, and a noon-lethargy curse. The None burrower goes for a patient's heart. A betrayal: a patron of the hospice turns out to be a Choir acolyte.
5. **Ch5 "Vespers and Compline": the city as patient.** Hollow Night. Kreuzer is put on trial (the fair-trial rule; witch-pricking evidence). Vespers turns blood to wax across the ward, then Compline, which steals the Litany itself. Endings depend on Stroh's trust, Kreuzer's Whisper meter, and whether he signed any false decree-examinations.

## 8. Operations, ailments, and bosses bridging Trauma Center with this tone

Tools: Lancet, Tongs, Leech-Pipe, Gut Thread, Saint's Salve, Tincture, Cautery Brand, Scrying Lens, and the Litany. "→" gives the core mechanic.

### 8.1 Mundane operations (Ch1 register, reusable later)
1. **"Ball and Wadding"** (lead shot). The ball carried scraps of doublet cloth into the wound. → Lens reveals 2–4 cloth fragments; any fragment left in causes a delayed "wound-fever" phase. Based on Paré-era gunshot practice ([Paré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambroise_Par%C3%A9)).
2. **"The Crow's Beak"** (amputation). A shattered shin after a cart crush. → Choose to seal with the Cautery Brand (fast, painful, lowers vitals) or ligate with Gut Thread (slower, higher score). Teaches the historical ligature-over-cautery breakthrough.
3. **"Quarrel at the Gorget"** (crossbow bolt near the carotid). → Clamp with Tongs *before* extraction, or arterial spray adds bleeding every second (like 4e *Arterial Damage*).
4. **"The Most Hated Avocation"** (dentistry). A jaw abscess, and the patient bites. **Make the patient Inquisitor Stroh.** A trust-building comic op: extract the rotten tooth without breaking the root while Stroh interrogates you between groans.
5. **"The Pieman's Revenge."** Gut worms from a penny pie. → Drain the flux, pull worm heads out whole (a torn worm regrows), antiparasitic Tincture. Black humour in the briefing.
6. **"Woolsorter's Blisters."** Black-crusted sores from infected fleeces. → Excise without scattering spores (spores spread if you drag the Lancet across them), then seal with Salve.
7. **"The Flagellant's Back."** Nail-studded scourge wounds festering. The penitent thrashes, believing pain is holy. → Stabilise with Tincture to calm him before each extraction, or the field shakes.
8. **"Lance the Buboes"** (plague). → Lance each bubo cleanly (a sloppy lance becomes a festering wound, as in 4e), drain, and keep the pus off open cuts.

### 8.2 Monsters and magic (Ch2 escalation)
9. **"Tusk and Hoof."** A convoy guard gored by a horned forest-dweller. → The horn tip is broken off inside; ticks crawl out of the wound and burrow if not plucked in time. Dung contamination lowers vitals until irrigated.
10. **"Gravedigger's Scratch."** A ghoul claw. → Dark infection lines climb toward the shoulder in real time; drain and dose Tincture at each branch point. If a line reaches the armpit, forced amputation (*Crow's Beak* reprise).
11. **"Troll-Blood Sellsword."** A mercenary drank a black-market regeneration draught. → Wounds close over embedded shrapnel within seconds; you must brand each wound's rim to stop regrowth before extracting. Phase 2: opening the gut sprays acid that corrodes a tool (disabled for 10 s).
12. **"The Assassin's Kiss."** Venom from a poisoned stiletto. → Venom motes travel along drawn veins; cut them off with Gut-Thread ligatures, drain with the Leech-Pipe, and match colour-coded antidotes (like Tetarti).
13. **"The Cunning-Woman's Knot."** A hedge-witch's curse-sigil wrapped round a nerve (existing *Sigil* entity). → Trace the sigil *backwards* in stroke order; cutting it raw makes it snap and whip out lacerations.
14. **"Hexstone Shot."** A mutagenic ball from a Choir marksman. → Tissue around the ball buds teeth, fingers and eyes. Excise buds before they root (root timer), and lift the stone only with Tongs into a lead dish (touching it adds Whisper).
15. **"The Stone Bride."** Petrification spreading from a bride's fingertips toward her heart (inspired by tomb-stiffening and life-magic petrification). → Crack plates with Lancet taps in a pattern and Salve the living margin. The Litany freezes the spread. The Lens shows the "stone front".
16. **"The Dead Man's Pulse."** A noble's "corpse" arrives for a forensic exam, and the Lens shows one heartbeat per minute. → Branch: operate to save a victim of a bite-trance, or confirm to Stroh that it is an undead thing. Forensic/inquisition discipline prototype.
17. **"Swain's Neck."** A courtesan with repeated bite wounds, tooth fragments lodged, and anaemia. She begs you to stop. → Extract fragments, then choose whether to cauterise the bite channel (ending the thrall) or leave it.
18. **"The Hornchild"** (Ch2 moral set piece). A child's skull shows horn-buds. Stroh invokes the inspection decree. → A trepanation-style excision (drill, lift, Salve), **or** refuse and sign the certificate "natural growth". Either way the Choir's sigil is under the bud. Consequence flags carry forward.

### 8.3 Later chapters
19. **"Delver's Lung."** A mountain-folk miner with crystal nodules and a false-recovery euphoria stage (3-stage sickness). → Remove nodules before stage III; don't shave the beard.
20. **"The Swallowed Strongbox."** A giant mercenary swallowed stolen evidence. → Extra-thick incision layers, a low organ layout (Lens required), heavy retraction with Tongs, and a lock you must pick *inside* the stomach.
21. **"Choir-Throat."** A chorister's larynx grows extra vocal folds that hum the hymn. Audio hazard: the hum spreads "motes" to the surgeon's field and mutes your audio cues. → Excise the folds in time with the silence between verses.
22. **"The Mouth Beneath."** A talking abdominal cyst that bargains and threatens to tell Stroh your secret (VN overlay mid-op). → Remove it whole; if you rupture it, it becomes a crawling remnant boss.
23. **"Blood of Tallow."** A chandler's daughter whose blood is setting like wax. → Drain waxy clots, re-warm vessels with the Brand on low heat, and give Tincture. Leads into Vespers.
24. **"Under the Witch-Moon"** (field obstetrics, handle with care). Midwives are trying to delay a birth until dawn out of superstition. → Kreuzer must overrule them. Historical hook: the first *reported* Caesarean on a living mother (c. 1500) is attributed to a Swiss sow-gelder, Jakob Nufer; the account is disputed. Treat it as a hope-giving operation.

### 8.5 Systems suggested by the research
- **Symptom Loom (procedural ailments and challenge mode).** Diseases are made from 3 adjacent "verses" out of ~12 symptom modules (buboes, flux, fever, pox, gangrene, convulsions, "coughs" that spread to adjacent patients in triage, and so on). This follows 4e's symptom pool and TW3's three-symptom plague cauldron (§2.2, §3).
- **Whisper meter** (suspicion plus corruption). Raised by Litany overuse, touching hexstone, and falsifying inspection certificates. It drives Stroh's scenes and the pricking minigame (§5).
- **Kessendorf districts.** Zero-sum Choir influence against mercy influence. The between-chapter map picks patients (TW corruption, §3).
- **Environment modifiers.** Candle-only light, a field tent in rain, an operating table in a moving cart. This builds on 1e's "surgery in a cave during combat" penalty (§2.2).
